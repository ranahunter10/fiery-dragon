# A full disk sheds records in order and never ends the run (F-02, 2026-11-14)

The design review's F-02 (`docs/audits/2026-11-13-design-review-dragon.md` §2): a WAL write error ended p15d with `P15_EXIT_IO`, and nothing capped, reserved or restarted. A full disk ended capture for the rest of the boot; F-01's flood fills a disk at the disk's own speed. This note records what the tools do, what was built, what it costs, and what is still open. Grades as in `CONTRIBUTING.md` §5.

## 1. What the tools do

| Tool | Before full | At full | Grade |
|---|---|---|---|
| auditd 3.1.2, `auditd.conf(5)` | `space_left` (a warning action), `admin_space_left` (a stronger action, often SUSPEND or SINGLE) | `disk_full_action` (IGNORE, SYSLOG, ROTATE, EXEC, SUSPEND, SINGLE, HALT); `disk_error_action` for write errors. SUSPEND stops writing records but keeps the daemon alive | DOC |
| systemd-journald (v253, `journald.conf(5)`) | `SystemMaxUse=` (10 % of the filesystem, capped at 4G) and `SystemKeepFree=` (15 %, capped at 4G); the smaller limit wins, enforced by deleting **archived** files | "will stop using more space, but it will not be removing existing files to reduce the footprint again" | DOC |
| Falco, Tetragon, Tracee | write events to an output (stdout, a file, gRPC, a ring); none keeps a durable log of its own that it must protect | the output's problem | SRC (no WAL in their code at 0815626, 75b95a2, 2f9dc40) |

Two lessons:
- **Tiers before full** (auditd's two thresholds, journald's keep-free). A daemon that only reacts to ENOSPC has no room left for its own account of what happened.
- **Stay alive.** auditd's SUSPEND keeps the daemon; journald stops growing. Neither exits.

Neither deletes the newest data. journald deletes the oldest archived files; auditd can ROTATE (also delete the oldest). Deleting old evidence is a retention policy, not a robustness fix, so it is left as a decision (§6, item 2).

## 2. What was built

`daemon/space.{h,c}` decides, `daemon/writer.c` counts and writes. Levels:

| Level | When | Written | Shed |
|---|---|---|---|
| OK | free ≥ low | every ring | nothing |
| SHED_BULK | free < low | spine, daemon | bulk (NET, file identity, FLOW) |
| SHED_ALL | free < reserve, or a batch was refused with ENOSPC/EDQUOT | daemon (STATS, WAL_SPACE, END) | spine and bulk |

- **Sizes.** `--wal-space-low-mb` and `--wal-space-reserve-mb`, 0 = auto: low = min(512 MiB, 10 %), reserve = min(64 MiB, 2 %, at least 4 MiB), and low ≥ 2 × reserve. Both are in the run header (`wal_space.low_bytes`, `wal_space.reserve_bytes`).
- **Hysteresis.** A level is left only above its threshold plus an eighth, so a disk at the edge does not flap.
- **No system call per batch.** `fstatvfs` once per writer loop; between readings every byte written lowers the estimate. A failed reading keeps the last one and is counted (`refresh_errors`).
- **ENOSPC is survived.** `p15w_append` truncates the segment back to its last whole batch, seeks back, counts `nospace` and returns the error; sequence numbers are not consumed, so the WAL stays valid. If the truncate itself fails, the writer fails as before (exit 4).
- **Rotation is held at SHED_ALL**: a new segment's header must not take the reserve.
- **The books.** `spine_shed` and `net_shed` are terms of the books' equations (`writer.c` `books()`), so a shed record is a counted loss, never a silent one. The run is DEGRADED from the first shed record, with the reason in the log.
- **The record.** WAL_SPACE (type 44, class STATS) at every change of level and with every STATS while the level is not OK or anything was shed: free and total bytes, the thresholds, the level, shed counts per ring and the spine's shed FORKs and EXITs (for the reader's process-tree check, `python/p15/reconcile.py`).
- **The closing snapshot** refused by ENOSPC makes the run DEGRADED, not INVALID.
- `--wal-space-guard 0` turns the tiers off; ENOSPC is still survived.

## 3. Why the order is bulk, then spine

The spine (FORK, EXEC, EXIT, RENAME) is the process tree every other record hangs from, and it is small: the soak's spine is a few percent of the WAL's bytes (E-0202). The bulk ring carries the volume and, under F-01's flood, almost all of it. Shedding bulk first keeps the tree whole longest per byte. The daemon ring is a few hundred bytes a second; the reserve holds it for hours (64 MiB at 1 KiB/s is about 18 hours, INF).

## 5. Costs

- Per batch: one compare (`p15_space_allows`) and one subtraction (`p15_space_wrote`). Per writer loop: one `fstatvfs` (a few hundred ns, INF; at most every `P15_WRITER_WAIT_MAX_MS`).
- Disk: the reserve and the low threshold are space p15d does not use for records but which other programs can still use.
- Not measured on the VM yet: V-2 (`docs/audits/2026-11-13-design-review-dragon.md` §6), now expected to end with exit 3, not 4.

## 6. Still open

1. **Who restarts p15d** after an exit (F-02 option 3): a systemd unit with `Restart=on-failure`, outside this change.
2. **Retention**: delete the oldest closed segments (journald) or keep everything and shed (this change). A policy decision.
3. **Another writer on the same filesystem** can still take the reserve. A dedicated filesystem or a quota for the WAL is the deployment answer (ADR-0013's threat model decides whether that attacker is in scope).
